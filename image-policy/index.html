Image policy

This page explains the two kinds of pictures used on BigCatWise and what the Source link under every photo and figure leads to.

Two kinds of images

BigCatWise uses two different kinds of pictures, and each is labelled so you can tell which is which.

  • AI generated photographs. Many of the photo style pictures beside our articles are AI generated images, made with an AI image tool. They are not photographs of real, named individual animals, real people or real events. They are illustrations of typical big cats and habitats, used to make pages easier to read. Each one carries a visible "AI-generated image." label directly under it.
  • Licensed photographs. Some photographs are genuine photos of real big cats, taken from Wikimedia Commons under a public domain (CC0) or Creative Commons Attribution ShareAlike licence. We check the licence on each file's own page before use, and credit the photographer in the caption and on our photo credits page.

The Source link

Every photo and AI generated figure on this site has a small "Source" link in its caption. For an AI generated image, that link leads back to this page. For a licensed photograph, the caption also names the photographer and licence, and the full source file, author and licence for every photo are listed on our photo credits page.

What AI images are not

AI generated images on BigCatWise do not show real, named animals, real people or real news events, and they carry no photographer credit. The facts on each page come from the sources listed at the bottom of that page, never from the images. For example, the article on tiger enclosure safety uses a generic AI image of a tiger and does not show the incident it describes, and our article on a tiger release in Kazakhstan uses generic AI images and does not show the real release.

Questions or corrections

If you think a photo is credited wrongly, or you have a question about an image, use our contact form and we will check it promptly. See also our copyright and image credits page.
