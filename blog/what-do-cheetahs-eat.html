What do cheetahs eat? What the research on cheetah diet and prey shows

By the BigCatWise team | Published 7 November 2026 | Last updated 7 November 2026 | Last reviewed 7 November 2026

Cheetahs mostly eat medium sized antelope. A review of 23 diet studies from six countries found that cheetahs prefer prey of about 23 to 56 kg, with a peak at about 36 kg, and that blesbok, impala, Thomson's and Grant's gazelles and springbok were significantly preferred (Hayward and colleagues, 2006).

Medium sized antelope come first

To find out which animals cheetahs actually prefer, rather than simply which ones they eat most, Matt Hayward and colleagues pooled data from 21 published and two unpublished studies across the cheetah's range. They compared how often each prey species was killed with how common it was locally, using a measure called Jacobs' index. A species killed more often than its abundance would predict counts as preferred; one killed less often counts as avoided (Hayward et al. 2006).

The result was a clear size window. Cheetahs preferred the most available prey at a site within a body mass of roughly 23 to 56 kg, with the most preferred weight around 36 kg. Prey outside that range was generally avoided (Hayward et al. 2006).

Which species do cheetahs prefer?

Five species were significantly preferred across the studies: blesbok, impala, Thomson's gazelle, Grant's gazelle and springbok (Hayward et al. 2006). Which one dominates depends on where a cheetah lives. On the plains of East Africa that means gazelles, while in southern Africa impala and springbok are more important. Cheetahs also take hares, young of larger antelope and other small animals when the chance arises.

Why such a narrow size range?

A cheetah is built for speed, not strength. It is light, with a small head and relatively small teeth, so large, dangerous prey carries a real risk of injury. Hayward and colleagues concluded that the cheetah's body appears to have evolved for medium sized prey that can be subdued with little risk (Hayward et al. 2006). There is a second advantage. Medium sized prey can be eaten quickly, before lions or hyenas arrive to steal it. Losing kills to these larger rivals is a constant problem for cheetahs, which is covered in our post on how cheetahs live alongside lions and hyenas.

Key takeaways

  • Cheetahs prefer prey of about 23 to 56 kg, with a peak at about 36 kg (Hayward et al. 2006).
  • Blesbok, impala, Thomson's and Grant's gazelles and springbok were significantly preferred (Hayward et al. 2006).
  • Larger prey is generally avoided because the risk of injury is high for a light, fast cat.
  • Medium sized kills can be eaten before lions and hyenas take them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cheetah's favourite food?

Across 23 studies, blesbok, impala, Thomson's and Grant's gazelles and springbok were the prey cheetahs significantly preferred (Hayward et al. 2006).

How big are the animals cheetahs hunt?

Cheetahs prefer prey of about 23 to 56 kg, with the most preferred weight around 36 kg (Hayward et al. 2006).

Do cheetahs hunt large animals like zebra?

Rarely. Prey well above the preferred size range is generally avoided, because a light cheetah risks injury tackling it (Hayward et al. 2006).

Why do cheetahs eat so fast?

Lions and hyenas often steal cheetah kills, so medium sized prey that can be eaten quickly is an advantage (Hayward et al. 2006).
