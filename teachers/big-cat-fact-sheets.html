Lion fact sheet

Panthera leo

Weight110 to 272 kg
Head and body length137 to 250 cm, plus a 60 to 100 cm tail
LifespanAbout 12 to 18 years
Where it livesSub-Saharan Africa; Gir landscape, Gujarat, India
IUCN Red ListVulnerable

Four amazing facts

  1. The only truly social big cat: prides are built around related lionesses.
  2. Adult males have a mane; both sexes have a dark tail tuft.
  3. A roar can carry over several kilometres.
  4. About 22,000 to 25,000 adult and subadult lions in Africa (2025) and about 670 in India (2020).

Source: IUCN SSC Cat Specialist Group, summarised on BigCatWise lion facts. bigcatwise.com

Tiger fact sheet

Panthera tigris

Weight75 to 325 kg
Head and body length150 to 230 cm, plus a 90 to 110 cm tail
LifespanAbout 12 to 15 years
Where it lives10 countries in South, Southeast and East Asia and the Russian Far East
IUCN Red ListEndangered

Four amazing facts

  1. The largest cat and the only striped cat.
  2. Every tiger's stripes are unique, and its left and right sides differ.
  3. Tigers have a white spot on the back of each ear.
  4. About 3,726 to 5,578 wild tigers, not counting cubs.

Source: IUCN SSC Cat Specialist Group, summarised on BigCatWise tiger facts. bigcatwise.com

Leopard fact sheet

Panthera pardus

Weight17 to 90 kg
Head and body length91 to 191 cm, plus a 51 to 101 cm tail
LifespanAbout 13 to 21 years
Where it livesSub-Saharan Africa and scattered parts of Asia
IUCN Red ListVulnerable

Four amazing facts

  1. The most widespread big cat.
  2. Small rosettes, usually empty in the middle.
  3. Often stores its kills up in trees.
  4. Black panthers in Africa and Asia are melanistic leopards.

Source: IUCN SSC Cat Specialist Group, summarised on BigCatWise leopard facts. bigcatwise.com

Jaguar fact sheet

Panthera onca

Weight36 to 148 kg
Head and body length110 to 170 cm, plus a 44 to 80 cm tail
LifespanUp to about 26 years recorded
Where it livesMexico to northern Argentina
IUCN Red ListNear Threatened

Four amazing facts

  1. The only big cat in the Americas.
  2. Large rosettes, usually with dots inside.
  3. Kills prey with a skull piercing bite and swims readily.
  4. About 57,000 to 64,000 jaguars in Amazonia alone.

Source: IUCN SSC Cat Specialist Group, summarised on BigCatWise jaguar facts. bigcatwise.com

Cheetah fact sheet

Acinonyx jubatus

Weight23 to 65 kg
Head and body length113 to 140 cm, plus a 60 to 84 cm tail
LifespanUp to about 14 years in the wild
Where it livesMainly southern and eastern Africa; Sahara; Iran
IUCN Red ListVulnerable

Four amazing facts

  1. The fastest land animal: wild cheetahs have been measured at about 93 km/h.
  2. Solid round spots and black tear marks from eye to mouth.
  3. Semi retractable claws grip like running spikes.
  4. It cannot roar; it purrs and chirps.

Source: IUCN SSC Cat Specialist Group, summarised on BigCatWise cheetah facts. bigcatwise.com

Snow leopard fact sheet

Panthera uncia

Weight30 to 50 kg
Head and body length90 to 120 cm, plus an 80 to 100 cm tail
LifespanAbout 10 to 20 years
Where it livesMountains of 12 countries in Central and South Asia
IUCN Red ListVulnerable

Four amazing facts

  1. Lives typically at 3,000 to 5,000 m.
  2. Smoky grey coat with open rosettes and a very long, thick tail.
  3. It cannot roar.
  4. About 7,446 to 7,996 snow leopards, including 2,710 to 3,386 mature adults.
